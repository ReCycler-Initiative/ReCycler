# Textiles

Proper sorting and recycling of textile waste are important for protecting the environment and conserving natural resources. Usable, intact, and clean clothes and household textiles should be sold or donated for reuse. Broken or worn-out textiles can be recycled as end-of-life textiles if such collection is available in your area. Otherwise, they belong in mixed waste.

**Sorting instructions:**

Always follow regional and collection-point-specific instructions.

**YES:**

- usable, intact, and clean clothing
- household textiles such as curtains, towels, and bed sheets
- accessories and belts
- bags and shoes

**NO:**

- dirty, wet, or moldy textiles
- broken clothes and textiles (unless end-of-life textile collection is available in your area)
- carpets, pillows, blankets, padded items, and soft toys
- underwear, socks, and tights

**Please note:**  
Only intact, clean, and usable textiles are suitable for collection. Pack them carefully in a sealed plastic bag or sack so that they stay dry and clean. Do not burden collection points with waste, as this causes extra costs and inconvenience.

**Collection and transport:**  
Usable textiles can be taken to clothing collection points, recycling centers, or charity collections. The nearest collection point can be found, for example, through the Kierrätys.info service. End-of-life textile collection points are available regionally, and their locations can be checked with your local waste management company.

**Processing:**  
Collected textiles are sorted according to usability. Clothes in good condition are directed to reuse through stores or charitable organizations. End-of-life textiles are processed mechanically or chemically, producing recycled fiber for the manufacture of new products.

**Utilization:**  
Reusable textiles are sold domestically or abroad. Recycled fiber can be used to make insulation, acoustic panels, filter fabrics, and new yarns. In this way, the amount of textile waste is reduced and natural resources are conserved.

**Additional information:**  
Textile recycling opportunities vary by region. Check local instructions and collection points on the website of your local waste management company or through the Kierrätys.info service. By recycling textiles correctly, you promote sustainable development and reduce environmental burden.
