# Vehicle batteries (lead-acid)

Many batteries contain hazardous substances. Take batteries to the proper collection point. Materials recovered from collected batteries are recycled into raw materials for new products or utilized for energy recovery.

The collection points shown in ReCycler are intended for household waste.

**Sorting instructions:**

Always follow regional and collection-point-specific instructions. The sorting instructions presented here are general in nature and based on current common practice.

**YES:**

- vehicle batteries
- batteries from cars, vans, and trucks
- batteries from mopeds and motorcycles
- batteries from three- and four-wheeled vehicles and light quadricycles
- batteries from tractors, powered machinery, and off-road vehicles
- batteries from machinery built on a car chassis
- batteries from towed equipment and non-motorized vehicles
- trailer batteries
- batteries from motorhomes, museum vehicles, and service vehicles
- batteries for emergency exit lights and backup power systems
- forklift batteries
- cleaning machine batteries
- boat batteries

**NO:**

- portable rechargeable batteries and household batteries → take to battery collection
- lithium batteries

Batteries can also often be returned to the same store where they were purchased, or to another retailer in the same line of business. Batteries are transported from collection points to processing facilities.

At the processing facility, batteries are dismantled so that the materials can be recovered.

Nearly 100% of the lead can be recovered and used, for example, in the manufacture of new batteries. The plastic is recycled into material for battery casings. Some components can be utilized for energy recovery in waste-to-energy plants.

Lead-acid batteries can be identified by the **Pb** marking on the battery. The key component of a lead-acid battery is lead, which is a heavy metal. Leaving a used battery in nature harms the environment.

If you have a vehicle battery, select **“Vehicle batteries (lead-acid)”** in the ReCycler service to view recycling points.
