# Mixed waste

Mixed waste, also known as dry waste, combustible waste, or waste for incineration, consists of materials that cannot be recycled or do not belong to other waste fractions. It is important to sort waste carefully so that as little recyclable or hazardous material as possible ends up in mixed waste.

**Sorting instructions:**

Always follow regional and collection-point-specific instructions.

**YES:**

- dirty paper, cardboard, and plastic packaging (if plastic packaging collection is not available)
- incandescent and halogen bulbs
- fuses
- cleaning waste and vacuum cleaner dust bags
- waste containing PVC plastic (for example inflatable toys, plastic tarpaulins, oilcloths, rainwear)
- aluminum-coated plastics (for example crisp bags and coffee packages)
- ash and cigarette butts
- window glass, mirror glass, porcelain, ceramics, crystal, and heat-resistant glass (Pyrex)
- tapes (VHS and cassette tapes, as well as empty toner cartridges)
- printer cartridges
- photographs and negatives
- leather and rags
- rubber items (for example bicycle tires and toys)
- broken shoes and clothes
- ham fat and other liquid fat packed tightly (for example in a plastic bottle)
- disposable diapers and sanitary pads
- regular and mineral-based cat litter
- pet waste

**NO:**

- hazardous waste
- electrical devices
- materials that can be sorted for recycling

**Please note:**  
Burning waste at home is prohibited. In household fireplaces such as ovens and stoves, the temperature does not rise high enough, which leads to incomplete combustion and the formation of harmful emissions. Burning plastic may also damage or block the flue.

**Collection and transport:**  
Mixed waste is usually collected property by property. In more sparsely populated areas, collection may be arranged through regional collection points or shared bins used by several properties. Mixed waste is transported to waste-to-energy plants for energy recovery.

**Processing:**  
At a waste-to-energy plant, mixed waste is incinerated at a temperature of about one thousand degrees Celsius. The steam generated by combustion drives a turbine, and the plants produce electricity and district heat. Flue gases are cleaned using modern methods. Metals are separated from the slag produced, and part of the slag is used in earth construction, for example as cover material for landfills.

**Utilization:**  
The heat energy produced from mixed waste is used, for example, in district heating networks in cities and in industrial processes. In addition, waste-to-energy plants produce electricity for the national grid. Energy recovery from waste replaces fossil fuels and helps combat climate change.

**Additional information:**  
Effective sorting at the source ensures that as little recyclable or hazardous waste as possible remains in mixed waste. The energy content of mixed waste is largely renewable, and its use reduces greenhouse gas emissions. Energy recovery complements material recycling in accordance with European waste management practices. In countries where the rate of energy recovery from municipal waste is high, the level of material recycling is also generally high.

Sorting stations that accept mixed waste can be found in the ReCycler service.
