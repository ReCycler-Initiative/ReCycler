# Treated wood

Treated wood often contains chemicals that can harm the environment if it is not handled properly. That is why it is important to take all used treated wood to the correct collection points. This ensures that the hazardous substances in the wood can be managed safely.

The recycling points shown in the ReCycler service are intended especially for household waste.

**Sorting instructions:**

Always follow regional and collection-point-specific instructions. The following sorting instructions are general in nature and reflect current common practice.

**YES:**

- treated decking boards
- treated fences and posts
- pressure-treated construction timber
- old treated garden furniture

**NO:**

- untreated wood → belongs in wood waste
- non-treated wooden structures, such as furniture and plywood

Treated wood is generally not accepted as regular wood waste. However, many recycling points and waste stations do accept treated wood, and you can often return treated wood to the same place where it was purchased. Treated wood is processed at special facilities where energy can be safely recovered from it.

**Processing and recycling:**

Energy can be recovered from treated wood through incineration, but because it contains hazardous substances, its treatment requires strictly regulated processes. Leaving treated wood in nature is dangerous, as it may release environmentally harmful substances.

**Finding a recycling point:**

If you have treated wood to recycle, find the nearest recycling point in the ReCycler service by selecting the category **“Treated wood”** and checking the collection points available in your area.
