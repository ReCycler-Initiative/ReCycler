# Hazardous waste

Hazardous waste contains substances that may be harmful to people, the environment, or animals. For this reason, it must be sorted carefully and delivered to appropriate hazardous waste collection points. Sorting stations that accept hazardous waste can be found in the ReCycler service.

**Sorting instructions:**

Always follow regional instructions and the practices of sorting stations.

**YES:**

- paints, varnishes, glues, and solvents
- pesticides and chemicals
- oils and oil filters
- batteries and battery waste
- fluorescent tubes and energy-saving lamps
- medicines and mercury thermometers
- pressurized containers such as spray cans and gas cartridges
- poisons and disinfectants
- asbestos and other construction materials harmful to health

**NO:**

- household waste
- recyclable waste such as glass, metal, or cardboard
- ordinary electrical devices (WEEE)
- biowaste or mixed waste

**Please note:**  
Hazardous waste should be packed tightly in its original packaging or in a labeled container so that it does not pose a danger during transport and handling. Do not mix different hazardous substances together.

**Collection and transport:**  
Hazardous waste is accepted at separate hazardous waste collection points and sorting stations. In some areas, there are also mobile collection services, for which more information is available from the local waste management company.

**Processing:**  
Hazardous waste is handled in special facilities where it is neutralized, safely incinerated, or recycled into new raw materials. For example, metals and oils can be recovered and reused.

**Utilization:**  
Some hazardous waste can be safely recycled into raw materials for new products, while some is disposed of under controlled conditions so that it does not cause harm to the environment.

**Additional information:**  
Hazardous waste handling requirements may vary by region. Check local instructions and collection points on the website of your local waste management company or in the ReCycler service.
