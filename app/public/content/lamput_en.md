# Lamps

Lamps contain various materials such as glass, metals, and in some cases hazardous substances such as mercury. For this reason, it is important to take lamps to the correct collection points. Recycling lamps allows valuable raw materials to be recovered and helps prevent environmental harm.

The recycling points shown in the ReCycler service are intended especially for household waste.

**Sorting instructions:**

Always follow regional and collection-point-specific instructions. The following sorting instructions are general in nature and reflect current common practice.

**YES:**

- incandescent bulbs
- halogen bulbs
- energy-saving bulbs (contain mercury)
- fluorescent tubes and LED lamps
- compact fluorescent lamps (for example screw-base energy-saving bulbs)
- light bulbs for household fixtures

**NO:**

- broken glass items → belong in mixed waste
- light fixtures and lamp housings → belong in electrical equipment collection
- batteries and rechargeable batteries → belong in battery collection

Lamps must not be thrown into mixed waste, because many lamps contain substances that may be harmful to the environment. Energy-saving bulbs and fluorescent tubes may contain mercury, which is dangerous if released into nature. Lamps can often be returned to stores or collection points that have a separate collection container for lamps.

**Processing and recycling:**

When recycled, lamps are crushed and sorted. Glass parts, metals, and other materials are separated and sent for further processing. The mercury contained in energy-saving bulbs is recovered and handled safely. Glass materials can be reused, for example, in the manufacture of new lamps.

**Finding a recycling point:**

If you have lamps to recycle, find the nearest recycling point in the ReCycler service by selecting the category **“Lamps”** and checking the collection points available in your area.
