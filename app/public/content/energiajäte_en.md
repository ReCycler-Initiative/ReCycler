# Energy waste

Energy waste includes, among other things, packaging materials that are not suitable for packaging waste collection.

**Sorting instructions:**  
Always follow regional and collection-point-specific instructions. The sorting instructions below are general in nature and reflect current common practice.

**YES**

- plastic-based energy waste
- plastics marked with the recycling codes **01, 02, 04, 05, and 06** inside the triangle
- rinsed plastic food packaging (for example from cheese and cold cuts)
- containers for cultured milk and yogurt, butter and margarine tubs, stretch wrap and shrink wrap
- packaging containing both plastic and paper
- plastic canisters, tubs, buckets, lids, and caps
- plastic bags, sacks, and plastic film
- plastic bottles (for example soft drink and juice bottles; deposit bottles should be returned separately)
- plastic disposable tableware, packaging trays, and containers
- small plastic items such as dish brushes and plastic pen casings
- Styrofoam (dirty Styrofoam goes in mixed waste, for example fish boxes)
- foam rubber / polyurethane foam (cut into four pieces)
- old photographs and negatives
- soft drink crates

**Cardboard and paper waste accepted as energy waste:**

- dirty or wet cardboard and paperboard
- wet-strength cardboard, carbonless copy paper, posters, candy wrappers
- gift wrap and packaging paper, sticky notes
- plastic- and wax-coated paper, address labels and other stickers, and their backing paper

**Wood waste accepted as energy waste:**

- tarred wood, wooden structures combined with plastic (for example polypropylene or polystyrene plastic)
- wood shavings and sawdust (may also be accepted in wood waste collection)
- wooden furniture (may also be accepted in wood waste collection)

**NO**

- PVC plastic (**code 03** inside the triangle, though the marking may be missing)
- cords, pipes, hoses, artificial leather
- adhesive plastic film, brown packing tape, some stickers
- latex gloves and other protective clothing
- binders, plastic sleeves, overhead projector transparencies
- plastic cards, for example credit cards
- plastic tarpaulins and plastic grates
- inflatable toys, air mattresses, and swim rings
- plastics used in construction, such as wallpaper, floor coverings, trim, and gutters
- raincoats, shower caps and shower curtains, shoe covers, aprons
- oilcloths, deep-drawn sales packaging (for example for tablets, toys, and car accessories)
- CD, DVD, and vinyl records
- non-packaging plastics marked with **code 07** inside the triangle
- other unidentified plastic objects

**Other waste not suitable for the energy waste fraction:**

- material containing aluminum (for example foil-lined chip bags, juice cartons, roasting bags)
- biowaste, items containing fluorine, Haltex boards, toothpaste tubes and toothbrushes
- tapes and cartridges (VHS tapes, cassette tapes, printer cartridges), ceramics, rubber, rotten wood, glass, metals, leather
- hazardous waste, pressure-treated wood, pressurized containers (for example hairspray, spray paint, deodorants)
- batteries, porcelain, wooden structures containing roofing felt, concrete
- dusty waste (for example cleaning waste, vacuum cleaner bags, ash, waste electrical and electronic equipment)
- light bulbs, diapers, sanitary pads

**Collection and transport:**  
Energy waste is collected through regional collection points and property-specific collection systems. In most cases, energy waste collection is subject to a fee.

**Identification color:**  
Orange (most commonly)

Energy waste is transported from collection points for the production of recovered fuel.

**Processing:**  
In recovered fuel production, the waste is shredded into a size suitable for combustion technologies. Metals are removed so that they do not interfere with the combustion process. In industrial-scale recovered fuel production, biodegradable material, fine mineral matter, metals, and heavy non-combustible material can be separated from the waste.

Recovered fuel made from energy waste can be burned as a mixed fuel in power plant boilers, heating boilers, or process kilns together with conventional fuel.

**Utilization:**  
Energy waste produces heat and electricity in co-combustion for industrial and residential needs.
