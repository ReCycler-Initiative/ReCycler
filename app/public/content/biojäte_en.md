# Biowaste

Biowaste consists of organic, biodegradable waste such as food scraps, peels, and plant material. When sorted correctly, biowaste can be used to produce compost or biogas, which reduces landfill burden and generates renewable energy.

The recycling points shown in the ReCycler service are intended especially for household waste.

**Sorting instructions:**

Always follow regional and collection-point-specific instructions. The following sorting instructions are general in nature and reflect current common practice.

**YES:**

- fruit and vegetable peels
- food scraps and spoiled food (including meat and fish)
- coffee grounds and filter bags
- tea bags and loose tea leaves
- eggshells
- paper towels and napkins (dirty)
- biodegradable biowaste bags

**NO:**

- plastic packaging → belongs in plastic recycling or mixed waste
- metal objects → belong in metal recycling
- glass → belongs in glass recycling
- medicines → must be returned to a pharmacy
- cigarette butts, diapers, and hygiene products → belong in mixed waste

Biowaste must not be placed in the biowaste bin in a regular plastic bag unless the bag is biodegradable. Proper sorting helps prevent odors, reduces methane emissions, and enables waste to be used beneficially, for example as fertilizer or as an energy source.

**Processing and recycling:**

Biowaste is transported to a processing facility, where it is turned into compost or biogas. Compost is used as a soil improver, and biogas is converted into electricity and heat. By composting at home, you can make use of biowaste in your garden and reduce the need for waste transport.

**Finding a recycling point:**

If you have biowaste, check which biowaste collection system is in use in your area. You can find biowaste collection points in the ReCycler service by selecting the category **“Biowaste.”**
